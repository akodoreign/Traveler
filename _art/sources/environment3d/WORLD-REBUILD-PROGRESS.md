## Cave entrances and doors

- Carve a real passage with measurable depth into the hillside or cave wall. Fit the jambs and ceiling into the surrounding rock, and carry the walkable floor through to the actual threshold. Check the opening from the front and side, walk through and back, and make sure neither flank bypasses a gated threshold.
- Keep the walkable threshold centered under the opening. Place the entry trigger where the party reaches it by walking straight ahead. Set the landing a clear step inside the destination, with the party facing away from the return trigger. Match the return direction to the route outside.

- Give enterable buildings visibly ajar leaves. Use closed doors for decorative facades and for doors waiting on a key, time, or story flag. Fit hinges, frames, steps, and railings to the measured opening and the ground on both sides.
- Extend castle curtain walls into terrain or a real perimeter. Fit gate collision to the visible posts and gate state. Test both the direct approach and the flanks.
- Try entry and return in the native game without jumping or doubling back. For gates and moving barriers, check the locked and opened states and any scripted opening. Check that companions and the camera have room at both landings.

## Working rules

1. Lead with readable shapes and a tight color palette. Inspect Comfy meshes for baked scenery, white backdrops, and stray geometry before staging them. Make structures such as doors and shelves from clean, separately modeled pieces when fit matters.
2. Use the actual doorway cut and floor grade for the door and stoop. Bind ajar leaves to live entrance events. Walk into and out of caves and doors using the native game so that the visible opening and trigger can be checked together.
3. Build and review daylight frames at 3840 × 2160 in the two isolated profiles. Check both gameplay distance and close angles. Keep animated details and background props outside the travel and formation lanes.
4. Use `install_environment_rows.py --preserve-existing` when publishing a new group. Keep existing full-pass rows and shared asset bytes intact. When upgrading a props-only row, carry its fitted placements and collision changes into the scoped terrain update. Archive the map receipt, art recipe, source assets, and selected daylight samples before moving to the next group.
5. Style Traveler's trees like miniature trees in a model railway diorama. Preserve each species' silhouette and foliage. The coastal pines illustrate the miniature finish. Use textured meshes directly for this finish, give the branches and canopy a gentle breeze, and add only a few drifting leaves to trees in fall color. `diorama_trees.py` contains the mesh and leaf setup.
6. Give boats the same miniature diorama finish. Fit the hull to the way the boat is moored and keep fine plank seams, rails, and tie-off details legible at gameplay distance.
7. Center the frame, step, and trigger on the actual doorway. On the ordinary two-block cut, the frame and step use the cut's left edge as their origin while the fixed door slab uses its center. Keep the hinge offset and place the portal at the center of the cut. Check floor grade on both sides.
8. Reuse finished Traveler props where they fit. Props are interchangeable, including textured 3D meshes such as the quarry tents and their animated flaps. Build each map's terrain for its own layout. Fit shared props to the ground and leave entrances and gameplay lanes clear. Furniture, screens, carts, and other detailed props can use continuous textured meshes. Fix gritty texture artifacts and preserve the placement and readable silhouette.
9. Use the richest, deepest, most brilliant colors the scene can carry. Accents follow the local clan. Snow can be luminous blue-white against nearly black timber and warm amber windows. The Shadowlands use eerie luminous accents, readable silhouettes, and deep color in the shadows. Only the Kuni Blighted Lands may use muted colors. Keep desaturated swatches in the reserve pile and out of active palettes elsewhere.

10. Use real stone scans and matching normal or bump maps wherever the geology benefits. Retint the diffuse into the scene's palette and preserve the stone's physical relief. Use warm Japanese lanterns and local fill light wherever the actual gameplay lighting buries a landmark. Check the result with the map's gameplay lighting as well as daylight.

11. Match combat arenas to the terrain where encounters start. Check the original encounter table and test a real encounter. Place water, walls, and props behind or around the fighting area so both formations stay readable.

12. Connect mine tracks through visible turnouts and switch stations. Fit the network to the gallery floor and finish serving tracks at loading areas or buffers. Give any deliberate break a visible cause such as a missing bridge or a collapse. Walk the connecting gallery in the game.
