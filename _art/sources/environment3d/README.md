# Traveler environment rebuild

This directory holds the ComfyUI recipes, accepted material references, editable voxel sources, map layouts and native capture scripts. The game loads the published meshes and textures from `_tools/rpgcobo-tool/project/resource/native_environment`. The environment index binds assets to specific maps and `RokuganEnvironmentKit.sk` handles motion in the game scene.

The repository-wide scope is recorded in `inventory/map-catalog.csv` and `inventory/voxel-catalog.csv`. The initial scan counted 697 map worlds and 620 existing environment voxel models. These counts include registered content, debug worlds and legacy stock models. Use the location and existing event data to identify which replacement belongs on each map.

Where the story places an entrance in a cliff, cut a walkable passage into the actual world, leave room for the party under the roof, and expose the floor and sidewalls deep enough to see inside. Keep the entrance interaction reachable from the approach. The first cuts span seven blocks at the mouth and extend fourteen blocks into the rock. Their day and night layouts use the same footprint.

The native playtest walked under the roof on M102, M105 and M437. The unlocked Egg Caves portal spans the passage beneath the roof on the day and night maps. Both routes reach M130 on a forward walk. Compact door portals stay active outside the camera view and have a clearance gap at their return landings. The trigger changes live in the map event data and its generator. `recessed-caves-world-build.json` records the terrain build.

When a continuous road crosses a boundary, land on the opposite edge of the next map. The eastbound road from M350 now enters Kudo from the west along a raised lane between the docks. The Kudo market ward retains its original north entrance. `tools/edgecheck.py M350 M351 M640` reports no mismatches in this connection. The first wider Scorpion audit identified four other directed transitions that need separate review with their adjoining trail or cave layouts.

The four M555 set models share a deck origin at [37.25, 2.50, 29.75] in world units. The two transition panels are 9.10 m wide and 6.35 m high. Their centers close to x=32.75 and x=41.75 at z=27.70. The palace frame for The Title occupies the back of the deck. The Blade set leaves the central and audience-right confrontation area clear. Use `kabuki-set-layout.json` for the prop layout.

Fit an ajar door to an actual entrance event. Its visibility follows that event's real unlock requirements. Fit closed leaves to decorative facades. Match each stone stoop to the facade sill and the grade directly outside. For the rebuilt interiors, choose construction and furnishings for the room's purpose and keep the story events and aisle in place. The shared small candlestick model `candle1` now uses two new paper lantern shapes for its lit and unlit states. Dedicated candle and torch variants use their original models until their themed passes.

## Rebuild and verify

1. Run the matching Comfy recipe. Use the receipts to recover jobs already queued. Hunyuan jobs are local to port 8189 and image jobs use port 8188.
2. Run the matching `build_*.py` script with the Traveler worktree path. Recipes importing Trimesh and SciPy use the local Comfy 3D Python environment. Run Coast surface prep first on a fresh staging folder to supply the shared textures, fixtures and environment index.
3. Run `build_cave_entrance.py` after both the Coast and Oracle recipes. It uses the world generator's playable tunnel cut to rebuild the exposed rock surfaces. Copy the generated native meshes, textures and referenced entries to the production resource folder. Preserve entries from the other maps. If a map's physical layout changes, compare the existing event data and navigable entrances before compiling its world file.
4. Run `run_preview.py <Traveler worktree> <map id>` to render the game scene at native 3840×2160. `--waves` captures extra animation frames for Coast Road or the Furnace battle. `--entrance` walks a party underneath the new cave roof before capturing. The script launches its own test game and waits for other engine sessions to release the single-instance lock.

## Architecture references

- Castle foundations and approach stonework. [Shiga Prefecture's Azuchi Castle guide](https://www.pref.shiga.lg.jp/sd00/2516.html)
- Timber framing and early castle details. [Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism's Inuyama Castle guide](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/common/001561398.pdf)
- Plaster roofs and defensive details from the early 1600s. [UNESCO's Himeji-jo listing](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/661/)
- Interior finishes and display furniture. [The Met's shoin room](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/640986), [Kyoto National Museum's sixteenth-century lacquer chest](https://www.kyohaku.go.jp/eng/collection/meihin/urusi/item06/)

### Dragon mountain inn

M380 and M381 use a warm cypress, slate, clay and celadon palette. Keep grain subtle and use the silhouette, joinery and material edges to distinguish pieces from normal gameplay distance. Use separate meshes for the door leaf and frame. Place the frame from the actual two-block doorway cut, place the stoop from the grade below it, and drive the leaf from the existing portal. Build shelves with open compartments and leave enough space around jars for their outlines to read. Test the approach and return from the inn, then inspect the prop at gameplay distance and in a close daylight capture. The other Dragon mountain maps remain in the test build.
